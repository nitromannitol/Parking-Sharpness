# Parking-Sharpness

A Lean 4 formalization of
> **Sharpness and critical scaling of parking**  
> Ahmed Bou-Rabee and Christoforos Panagiotis, [arXiv:2609.02820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02820)

The paper's theorems, lemmas, propositions and corollaries are stated in Lean
and proved from Mathlib and the shared library
[Lattice-Probability](https://github.com/nitromannitol/Lattice-Probability),
which supplies the lattice, the simple random walk operator, i.i.d. fields and
the particle–hole process the model is built on.  Results the paper quotes from
other papers are not proved here: each is stated as a proposition in
`Parking/External/` and enters the theorems that use it as an explicit
hypothesis.

## The model

At each site of the lattice ℤ^d an independent random integer η(x) is placed,
the number of particles at x minus the number of holes there (in the parking
model each site holds one car or one spot).  In each round every unsettled
particle takes one step of a simple random walk, and it settles when it reaches
a hole that is still unfilled.  The particle odometer U_n(x) is the number of
steps taken from x in the first n rounds, and the divisible sandpile odometer
u_n(x) is its deterministic analogue, u_0 = 0 and u_{n+1} = (η + P u_n)⁺, where
P is the transition matrix of simple random walk.  The main theorem
(`thm:master`) says that if η(0) is nonconstant with mean zero and
E e^{θ|η(0)|} < ∞ for some θ > 0, then there are constants 0 < c ≤ C < ∞ with

```
c (E u_n(0) + log n)  ≤  E U_n(0)  ≤  C (E u_n(0) + log n)      for every n ≥ 2.
```

With the growth of E u_n(0) that the paper quotes, this gives E U_n(0) ≍ n^{(4−d)/4}
for d ≤ 3 and E U_n(0) ≍ log n for d ≥ 4 at the critical density (`cor:growth`).
The paper also proves a stretched-exponential bound with exponent d/(d+2) on the
settling time below the critical density (`thm:subcritical-tail`), the order of
divergence of E U_∞(0) as the mean of η(0) rises to zero (`thm:near`), and
results on the nearest hole and particle (`thm:nearest`,
`thm:nearest-counterexample`) and on the oriented walk (`thm:oriented-walk`).

## Terms

These words are used throughout with fixed meanings.

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| **registered statement**, also called a **node** | one Lean declaration transcribing one statement of the paper, or one result the paper cites. Each has an entry in `ledger/manifest.yaml`. |
| **frozen statement** | the declaration's text between the lines `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-BEGIN` and `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-END`, pinned by the SHA-256 of those bytes. It cannot change without the change being recorded in the manifest. The proof after the end marker may be rewritten freely. |
| **`SEALED`** | the node is **proved** here: a complete proof, whose axiom closure contains only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. |
| **`FROZEN`** | the node is **assumed**, not proved: a result the paper quotes from the literature, stated as a proposition in `Parking/External/` and carried as an explicit hypothesis by every theorem whose proof uses it. Every one is listed with its statement in [`ASSUMPTIONS.md`](ASSUMPTIONS.md). |
| **`DRAFT_SORRY`** | the statement is pinned but its proof is still open, registered as a `sorry`. |
| **cited input**, **external** | a `FROZEN` node, in the sense above. |
| **axiom closure** | the axioms a proof ultimately rests on, as reported by Lean's `#print axioms`. |
| **`sorryAx`** | the axiom Lean inserts for an unproved `sorry`; its presence in an axiom closure means the result is not proved. |

The word *frozen* carries two senses, so it is worth separating them: every
registered statement is frozen in the sense of being **pinned by hash**, while
the state `FROZEN` marks the subset that is **assumed rather than proved**.

## How a statement is tied to the paper

Each registered statement is one Lean declaration in its own file, under
`Parking/Frozen/` for the paper's theorems, lemmas, propositions and corollaries
and under `Parking/External/` for cited results.  The statement sits between
the lines `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-BEGIN` and `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-END`.

`ledger/manifest.yaml` has one entry per such declaration.  An entry records the
paper location (`source`: the line range in `paper/parking.tex` and the LaTeX
label the statement transcribes), the file, the Lean name (`export`), the state,
and `frozen_sha256`, the SHA-256 of the bytes between the two markers.  The
manifest also records the SHA-256 of the pinned paper as `source_pin`.

The proof follows the end marker and may be rewritten freely, provided the bytes
between the markers do not change.  `tools/freeze.py` registers or refreshes an
entry, and `tools/paper_anchors.py` recomputes each recorded line range from its
LaTeX label.

Two checkers compare the two statements rather than the hashes.
`tools/check_clauses.py` counts the assertions of a paper statement (displayed
relations and enumerated items) and the top-level conjuncts of its Lean
conclusion, and requires a written reading of every node saying what each side
asserts; a node with no reading fails.  `tools/check_exponents.py` extracts the
exponents from both sides and matches them, following the definitions a Lean
statement names, since an exponent often lives in a definition rather than in
the statement.  A superscript that is a set name (`ℤ^d`), a decoration
(`f^{(R)}`) or an exponential is treated as such; anything else the paper has
and Lean does not must be explained per node or the check fails.

## Which Lean theorem proves which result

`CORRESPONDENCE.md` lists them.  Its section "Frozen surface" is a table, generated
from `ledger/manifest.yaml`, that gives for every registered statement its node id,
its Lean name, its line range and label in `paper/parking.tex`, and its state.  Its
section "External inputs" gives the source of several of the cited results and the
theorems that use them.

## Building and checking

The Lean toolchain is pinned in `lean-toolchain` and the Mathlib revision in
`lake-manifest.json`.  `lakefile.lean` requires Lattice-Probability from git at a
pinned revision, so `lake` fetches it together with Mathlib; there is nothing to
clone by hand.

```sh
elan toolchain install $(cat lean-toolchain)
lake exe cache get
lake build
```

The checkers need Python 3 and PyYAML.  `check_axioms.py` and `check_warnings.py`
run Lean; the others read the manifest, the paper and the Lean sources.

| Command | What it guarantees |
|---|---|
| `python3 tools/check_manifest.py` | Every node's file holds exactly one frozen block whose SHA-256 equals `frozen_sha256` and which declares exactly the node's `export`; every file in `Parking/Frozen/` belongs to one node; `Parking/` and `Parking.lean` contain no `axiom`, `admit` or `sorryAx`, and `sorry` occurs only in files of `DRAFT_SORRY` nodes |
| `python3 tools/check_axioms.py` | Lean's `#print axioms` is run on every manifest export, and the check fails if any axiom closure contains `sorryAx` |
| `python3 tools/check_warnings.py` | `lake build Parking` emits no Lean warning other than one `declaration uses 'sorry'` for each `DRAFT_SORRY` node |
| `python3 tools/check_constants.py` | In a frozen statement whose conclusion has a real-valued `∃`, none of `R n t m r s L x y u v e z ε η δ` is a theorem parameter, so the constant cannot depend on it |
| `python3 tools/check_coverage.py` | Every labelled theorem, lemma, proposition and corollary of `paper/parking.tex` is named in the `source` of some manifest node |
| `python3 tools/check_clauses.py` | Compares the assertion count of each paper statement with the conjunct count of its Lean statement, and requires a recorded reading of every node |
| `python3 tools/check_exponents.py` | Compares the exponents written in a paper statement with those in its Lean statement and the definitions it names |

`python3 tools/sync_docs.py --write` regenerates the manifest-derived blocks in
`README.md` and `CORRESPONDENCE.md`, and `python3 tools/certificate.py`
regenerates `CERTIFICATE.md`.  `python3 tools/assumptions.py`
regenerates `ASSUMPTIONS.md`.  `tools/paper_anchors.py` and
`tools/paper_citations.py` check the `parking.tex:<lines>` ranges in the manifest
and in the Lean docstrings against the paper's labels.

## Scope

**Formalized.**  Every labelled theorem, lemma, proposition and corollary of
`paper/parking.tex` is the `source` of a node in `ledger/manifest.yaml`
(`tools/check_coverage.py`).  The statements are for an arbitrary i.i.d.
integer-valued law ν on ℤ (`Parking.law d ν`), as in the paper; the parking
model is the case where each site is a car or a spot.

**Cited results taken as hypotheses.**  The 18 `FROZEN` nodes are:

- the growth of the mean divisible sandpile odometer (`ext-sandpile-growth`), the
  concentration estimate for it (`ext-u-concentration`) and the collected Green
  function rates (`ext-green-norms`), all from Bou-Rabee and Panagiotis's paper on
  the divisible sandpile;
- Pinelis's martingale moment and Bernstein inequalities (`ext-bernstein`);
- the Donsker–Varadhan estimate for the range of a random walk
  (`ext-donsker-varadhan`);
- the cutoff and stability estimates and the binomial local central limit theorem
  used for the oriented walk (`ext-oriented-stopping-stability`,
  `ext-binomial-local-clt`);
- the lower-tail estimate at the critical scale used for `thm:nearest`
  (`ext-critical-scale-lower-tail`) and the two hypotheses it carries
  (`ext-variance-scale`, `ext-multivariate-berry-esseen`);
- the eight inputs of the scaling limit of the odometer in `prop:spatial-scaling`
  (`ext-spatial-fixed-time-tightness`, `ext-spatial-stopping-stability`,
  `ext-srw-local-clt`, `ext-linear-field-scaling`, `ext-spatial-odometer-scaling`,
  `ext-heat-strong-minimum`, `ext-heat-interior-regularity`,
  `ext-heat-compactness`).

`Parking.Frozen.master`, for example, takes `hGrowth`, `hBernstein`,
`hConcentration` and `hGreenNorms` as hypotheses.

**Not formalized.**  The paper's proof-overview and related-work subsections and
its five remarks (`rem:transpose`, `rem:tilt`, `rem:stabilization-mixing`,
`rem:first-moment`, `rem:critical-dimension`) are not registered nodes.  Its two
figures, snapshots of the process on ℤ², are not formalized, and the image files
are not part of `paper/`.  The nearest-hole-and-particle question is answered for
d ≤ 3 by `thm:nearest` and for d ≥ 5 by `thm:nearest-counterexample`; neither
covers d = 4, where the paper says only that simulations suggest the
counterexample extends.

**Modelling decisions.**

- Distances on ℤ^d are graph distances (`parking.tex:588-601`), so the paper's |x|
  is `Parking.graphNorm x`, the ℓ¹ norm.
- The instruction stacks are indexed from zero here and from one in the paper.
- U_∞(x) is the supremum of U_n(x) in `ℕ∞`.
- The process is built two ways: the paper's stack construction, and a
  particle-driven construction in which every particle carries its own walk and
  uniform variables (`Parking/Support/Particle.lean`).  `lem:one-particle` and
  `lem:tagged-monotonicity` are stated for the particle-driven construction.
  `Parking.constructionsAgree` (`Parking/Support/Agree.lean`) proves that the two
  have the same law, which the paper asserts without proof at `parking.tex:645`.
- Other places where a Lean statement is not literally the paper's, and the
  statements the formalization repaired, are recorded in the sections "Where a
  Lean statement is not literally the paper's" and "Statement repairs" of
  `CORRESPONDENCE.md`.
